Sequential ordinary bootstrap

Let A_0 be a finite constant for the admitted initial ordinary algorithm with
saving a_0. At level j, the earlier algorithm and its constant A_(j-1) have
already been fixed. The admitted ordinary-wrapper theorem supplies the new
construction and its finite coefficients. Choose an integer C_j >= 1 which
bounds all its primitive, recurrence, wrapper, row, setup and padding
constants, its large-width constant, and the earlier ordinary constant. Such
a C_j exists under the admitted interfaces. It need not be at most 2^1920.

The saving is a_j = s*(1-s+a_(j-1)). Let delta_j be the minimum of its four
exact positive exponent gaps. Set B_j = ceil(log2(C_j)) and choose

  L_j = ceil(max(1, 36/delta_j^2, 2*(4+B_j)/delta_j)),
  e_j = 2^L_j.

For t = log2(e) >= L_j, use

  log2(1+t) <= 3*sqrt(t) <= delta_j*t/2,
  log2(C_j)+4 <= delta_j*t/2.

Then C_j*(1+log2(e))*e^(-delta_j) <= 1/16. This pays the lower
order terms and makes the high borrowed row fit. For e < e_j, use the earlier
ordinary algorithm. A finite constant which covers both ranges is

  A_j = C_j + A_(j-1)*2^ceil(L_j*(a_j-a_(j-1))).

This choice is sequential. C_j uses A_(j-1), while A_j is assigned after L_j.
No constant depends on itself. Six steps give the stated exponent a_6, with
finite constants and cutoffs whose numerical values are not certified by this
package. The exact saving exponents, their positive gaps, and the formula for
sufficient cutoffs are certified. The source's ordinary algorithm and its
uniform scheduling remain admitted interfaces.

The 1,920-bit supplier charge is separate from the stage constants C_j.
