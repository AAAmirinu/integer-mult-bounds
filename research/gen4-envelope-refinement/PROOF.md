# Finite envelope refinement of the reviewed gen4 supplier

This contribution reprices the construction reviewed at repository commit
`12a4934d15006e8c10f9b2264f13f8f341be802b`, whose selected witness is
`90446448750933/125000000000000000`. It changes finite rational envelopes and
finite ordinary-leaf/outer parameters. It does not change the scalar word,
frames, banks, charts, physical routing or the eligible-prime range. The
result is a conditional improvement of that checkpoint, not a claim to the
strongest pending submission or a new proof of the multiplication theorem.

The executable arithmetic certificate is generated by `pricing.py`. Exact
rational inputs and interval endpoints, rather than displayed decimal
approximations, determine each accepted witness. The certificate records
eleven investigation cycles, including rejected or non-improving trials.
Separate physical replay and source pins remain necessary: a histogram by
itself does not establish the construction that realizes it.

## The complete bad-class fallback can be charged more tightly

Write `N=2m`. The retained local-ring proof eliminates the full invertible
residual with unit pivots, not with field elimination that treats a
nonunit as zero. It bounds the operations by `N(N-1)` additions, `N` row
swaps and `N-1` paired diagonal scalings, together with paid nonrecursive
adapters. Charging every addition as an upper addition costs at most six
recursive children; a swap costs at most three; a paired diagonal scaling
costs at most six. Thus the stated bound before rounding is

```
F = 6N(N-1) + 3N + 6(N-1) = 24m^2 + 6m - 6.
```

For `m=120`, this is exactly `346314`, whereas the historical envelope
rounds it up to `32m^2=460800`. Every one of these children has relative
width at most `1/m`, including the tail reversals. Charging the smaller
tail children as full `1/m` children remains an overestimate. There is no
claim that `346314` is optimal, and no fallback branch or nonrecursive
matrix-preparation work is removed. The original `finite_check.py`
independently recomputes exactly this unrounded expression.

The coefficient-preparation, wrapper, selector and routing bills remain
the original conservative bills. Tightening the recursive upper bound
does not require a reduction to any of those finite constants.

## A uniform bad-class probability with the same prime range

Use `p` for the actual fixed eligible prime, to avoid confusing it with
the outer assembly's parameter also named `q`. Set `Q=2^80`. The retained
condition is `p>Q`, with every current source-specific determinant and
denominator exclusion still enforced.

For each fixed rank-`r` projector, the normalizer tests leading minors.
Their cleared numerators are nonzero polynomials once the fixed rank is
preserved modulo `p`; similarity to `diag(I_r,0)` provides a simultaneous
nonzero witness. The retained proof bounds their total degree by `m^3`.
Consequently a uniformly chosen `m` by `m` matrix is in the union of
their zero sets with probability at most `m^3/p`. This is a union over the
minor tests for one edge type, not over all edges in the physical word.

The low-class distribution is uniform on `GL_m(F_p)`, so conditioning
must be paid. Its probability within all matrices is

```
P_GL = product_{j=1}^m (1-p^(-j))
     >= 1 - sum_{j=1}^m p^(-j)
      > 1 - 1/(p-1) > 1/2.
```

The product inequality follows inductively from
`(1-x)(1-y)>=1-x-y` for `x,y` in `[0,1]`. It gives two admissible
uniform upper bounds on each edge type's conditional bad fraction:

```
b_1 = 2m^3/Q,
b_2 = m^3*(Q-1)/(Q*(Q-2)).
```

The first retains the old conditioning factor two but avoids rounding to
`10^-16`. For the second, first use
`m^3*(p-1)/(p*(p-2))`. The function

```
(x-1)/(x*(x-2)) = (1/2)*(1/x + 1/(x-2))
```

strictly decreases on `x>2`; therefore substitution of the smaller `Q`
is conservative. Both bounds are rational, positive and below one. They
are uniform in atom width and parent descriptors under the same retained
low-class/weighted-compiler contract. No independence between the minor
events, edge types or bank assignments is asserted or required.

Each edge type is averaged over its complete low cover. Right-multiplying
by the fixed bank normalizers is a bijection of that cover, as used in the
bank proof. Consequently no union bound over the millions of physical
events, replicas or bank assignments enters this probability. A global
“all edge types simultaneously good” interpretation would be a different
and unjustified argument.

## Two conservative moments and the disjoint-branch refinement

Let `H_r` be the unchanged normalized histogram, `E=sum H_r`, and let
`0<c<1` be a proposed coarse saving, with `tau=1-c`. Define

```
G(c) = sum_r (H_r/W)*(r/m)^(1-c),
B(c) = (F*E/W)*(1/m)^(1-c).
```

The historical style of upper bound is `G(c)+b*B(c)`. Replacing `F` or
`b` by the tighter bounds just proved retains the complete fallback
charge and is valid without claiming any cancellation.

A further refinement uses the disjoint alternatives already described in
the retained proof. The low-class minor tests choose the good whole-rank
child or the full bad residual fallback before recursive dispatch. On a
bad class, the construction does not first execute the good recursive
child and then execute the full fallback. Fixed preprocessing and branch
selection remain in the finite nonrecursive bill.

For one rank `r`, put `g_r=(r/m)^(1-c)` and
`f=F*(1/m)^(1-c)`. If its actual bad fraction is `t_r<=b`, its recursive
contribution is at most `(1-t_r)*g_r+t_r*f`. The expression increases
with `t_r` because `F>=r^(1-c)`: indeed `F>=max(r)` and `0<c<1` imply
that inequality. Hence it is at most `(1-b)*g_r+b*f`. Summing the edge
contributions proves the stricter bound

```
mu_(1-c) <= (1-b)*G(c) + b*B(c).
```

This charges the complete fallback on every bad class. It avoids charging
the mutually exclusive good recursive call on that same class. Subtracting
`b*G` without the disjoint branch contract or without proving `f>=g_r`
would be invalid. These requirements are explicit certificate conditions.

The synthetic mixture is a power-moment upper bound, not a componentwise
upper bound on the actual child histogram: when `t_r<b`, there can be
more good children than the coefficient `1-b` suggests. No replacement
recurrence for an arbitrary unknown function `T` is asserted. The same
argument separately bounds the linear power moment because `F>=r`.
These two moment bounds are what the weighted induction uses after
substituting its proposed power-law majorant into the actual recurrence.

Both moments are strictly increasing in `c`, since every child ratio lies
strictly between zero and one and all coefficients are positive. Rational
logarithm/exponential interval bounds certify a proposed lower saving with
an upper moment endpoint below one, and exclude an adjacent coarse grid
point with a lower endpoint above one. Decimal root searches only propose
endpoints. They cannot certify a sign. The same positive coefficients
also imply the linear moment at `c=0` is strictly below one.

For the repriced coarse recurrence use its new strict gaps
`delta_tau=1-mu_(1-c)>0` and `delta_linear=1-mu_1>0`.
The unchanged nonrecursive toll has the form
`C*n+C*w^(1-a_old)`. Increasing its fixed constant as needed, the
same weighted induction takes
`A=C*(1+1/delta_linear+1/delta_tau)` and bounds the recurrence by
`A*(n+n^(1-c)*w^(1-a_old))`. The linear and stopped gaps pay the
two tolls separately; floors only decrease child sizes. A certificate
for the old coarse saving cannot supply the new `delta_tau`: these
two gaps are recomputed for each refined moment. The physical maximum
child remains `50/120<1/2`, so a single recursion level halves the
size; this changes neither the one-level histogram nor the inherited
row-restoration argument.

## Finite ordinary-leaf composition

Fix one certified coarse rational `c`, rather than an unattained moment
root. Start with the retained completed ordinary supplier of saving
`a_0=384599/10^10`. For a finite depth `d`, define `A_j` only from
`A_(j-1)` at its ordinary leaf, routing, high-part and remainder calls;
its coarse recursive children still decrease their atom count. Select
atom exponent `theta=c`. The retained ordinary wrapper gives

```
a_(j+1) = (1-c)*c + c*a_j,
c-a_j = c^j*(c-a_0),
a_j < a_(j+1) < c < 1-a_(j+1).
```

The new completed interface still handles arbitrary widths, complete
spectators and restored rows. Its own high-digit borrowing is internal.
It does not request a new externally supplied row stock from its parent.
Radix padding and the fixed number of tapes multiply only across the
chosen finite depth. No level calls itself as its ordinary leaf, no
infinite-depth limit is instantiated, and `a_d=c` is never substituted.

At each step the finite absorption gaps are

```
c-a_(j+1),
1-a_(j+1)-c,
1-a_(j+1)-c*(1-a_j),
1-c.
```

They are strictly positive. Choose an integer `C_full>=1` dominating the
already fixed primitive, complete fallback, recurrence, wrapper, row,
setup, padding and preceding ordinary-level constants at this step. If
`delta` is the least gap, the existing cutoff rule is

```
L = ceil(max(1,36/delta^2,2*(4+ceil(log2 C_full))/delta)),
e_0 = 2^L.
```

For `e>=e_0`, the inherited estimate
`C_full*(1+log2(e))*e^(-delta)<=1/16` absorbs the lower-order tolls and
fits the borrowed high part. Below this finite cutoff use the preceding
completed ordinary algorithm. The next constant can be increased by the
finite factor `2^(L*(a_(j+1)-a_j))`, rounding upward if an integer
constant is wanted. Choose these constants sequentially in increasing
`j`; there is no circular dependence or input-dependent depth.

A numerical cutoff computed from only the explicit recipe coefficient is
not a numerical bound on all inherited implementation constants. The
theorem uses the displayed quantified `C_full`, as the reviewed finite
bill already requires. Additional depth can make these constants and
cutoffs enormously larger; this is not a practical runtime claim.

## Retained outer assembly and its ceiling

Let `a=a_d` and let `b_complex` be the unchanged independently checked
complex supplier. Use the retained outer function with positive rational
`eta` and `beta`, subject to all of its strict checks, especially
`a<(1-beta)*b_complex`. Its binding margin is

```
q_outer = a*(1-2*eta),
M = (1-eta)*q_outer/(1+q_outer).
```

A rational `kappa<M` passes only if all 47 strict constraints and seven
margins pass. For a denominator `D`, the largest grid point strictly below
`M` is `(ceil(D*M)-1)/D`; this matters when `D*M` is an integer. The
adjacent grid point must fail the binding strict inequality.

Increasing finite `a` increases `M` for `0<eta<1/2`; decreasing positive
`eta` also increases it. Changing `beta` alone does not change this
binding formula while the leaf-order inequality remains satisfied. It is
therefore reasonable for a beta trial to produce no improvement. All
accepted witnesses obey

```
kappa < M < a/(1+a) < c/(1+c).
```

Neither zero backoff nor the limiting coarse saving is an admissible
finite witness. Deeper bootstraps can also stop improving a fixed decimal
grid. The cycle record distinguishes these non-improvements from strict
improvements and does not imply a new parameter ceiling.

## Verification and adverse cases

The checker must bind the histogram, exact selected source package and
freshly replayed physical outputs; all fallback and finite charges above
must be computed from those values. It must preserve the original
complex-side calculation and report its retained assumptions. It should
check, with exact arithmetic:

- both independent interval engines accept the chosen coarse endpoint and
  exclude the next coarse grid point;
- the fallback formula includes the swap and diagonal terms, every bad
  fraction is positive and below one, and `Q>=2^80`;
- disjoint accounting is allowed only when the full fallback dominates
  every good child and the stated branch contract is retained;
- the finite recurrence and its independent closed form agree at every
  level, with positive absorption gaps and fixed finite depth;
- all 47 outer constraints and seven margins hold and the next final
  grid point fails.

Useful adverse controls reject an understated fallback count, a probability
below the proved envelope, an ineligible prime threshold, zero outer
backoff, a coarse root above its independently enclosed threshold, the
unattained assignment `a_d=c`, a detached ordinary saving, and a final
headline at or above its binding margin. A disjoint-moment diagnostic
with fallback cheaper than a good branch must reject the monotonic
substitution of `b` for an unknown actual bad fraction. These are checks
of mathematical failure modes, not evidence of a complete theorem proof.

The new envelope and mixture argument is written reasoning at the same
conditional level as the retained compiler. Finite arithmetic verification,
source identity and fresh physical replay have separate scopes. None is
described as independent human peer review or a practical benchmark.
