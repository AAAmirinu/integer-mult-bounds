## General sufficient theorem

Consider a finite linear network with immutable sources x_s, mutable helpers h, and observable targets y. Suppose its source injections are affine forcing events h_d <- h_d + x_s. For any potential event position i and destination d, let R(i,d) be its transported target-response vector: an injected scalar at that position changes final y by R(i,d) times that scalar.

Replace a set of source-s injections S by another set T. If sum_{i in S} R(i,d_i) = sum_{j in T} R(j,d_j), the final target transfer is unchanged for every source and every dirty initial helper/target. This follows by linear superposition: the difference of the two networks is the common immutable source x_s multiplied by the difference of the two response sums. The internal helper linear map is unchanged by changing source-only injections. Thus the dirty-helper contribution to the target transfer also remains unchanged. If all centre/delivery gates write only targets, reversing the modified helper network restores every helper and source. Source-only changes are essential: an analogous assertion for arbitrary mutable-source gates would need additional terms.

The response criterion gives an algebraic endpoint certificate only. Every actual rewrite must separately check frame monotonicity, source support, helper support, centre semantics and the physical invocation/owner contract. Those finite local checks are performed here; the parent campaign owns the complete native/global invoice and analytic composition.

For the realized witness the proof simplifies further. Between the new position and old position, helper452 is never read; its only intervening appearances are additions into it from helpers866 and243 at frame821. Adding immutable source5 commutes with those additions. The early injection is admissible at frame480 because source5 is in that frame. The delayed frame836 visit is therefore unnecessary. We describe this as a newly admitted retiming witness, not as invention of commutativity or a universal new coding theorem.
